📋 Executive Summary

A lighter week overall — total −7.4% to 1,032. Support rose slightly to 259 (+3.2%, email +52.2% to 70), while Collections fell −10.5% to 773. The apparent +97% jump in Collections spam is an artifact — last week's spam was undercounted due to a tagging gap (see the tag caveat below).

Two metrics went the wrong way. SLA missed target at 82.3% (−8.8 pp, below the 90% target), with average resolution time up to 92.2 min and 21 tickets still open — so SLA may fall further as they close. Deflection dropped to 48.6% (−9.9 pp, far below the 70% target); this week's tagging is healthy (~88%), so the drop is real — driven by boleto renegotiation (28 tickets, mostly handled by humans) and credit-limit increases.

On satisfaction the picture split. Support CSAT was 100% (27 of 27 positive, no Support detractors) on a solid response base. But Collections CSAT dropped to 76.2% (16 of 21, −17.4 pp) — all five detractors were buyers in the boleto self-service lookup flow (one CNPJ also rated low the week before), pointing again to friction in that automated journey rather than agent handling.

Q3 is now complete. The quarter finished with Support CSAT 94.8% (just under the 95% target), SLA 90.3% (above target), and deflection 55.6% (below the 70% target). Collections handled 9,786 tickets (+11.5% vs Q2) at 86.1% CSAT. Q4 has just started (3 days); preliminary figures are noted below the QTD tables.

No support requests were opened in #general-support this week (Evelyn out of office), so the Operations section is empty. On data quality: the previous week's (09-20) tagging gap is not backfilling (stuck near 70%), so that week's spam and L2 figures remain unreliable; this week's tagging is back to ~88%, so this week's deflection drop is real rather than a measurement artifact.

Reminder — ticket handling best practices (Collections)

As Collections agents start responding to tickets directly, a few practices keep the queue clean and the data reliable:

  • Keep an eye on your queue and respond to the tickets assigned to you.
  • When a case is resolved, make sure to mark the ticket as Resolved.
  • If you'll handle a case outside Chatwoot (e.g. from your own WhatsApp number), let the customer know via the Chatwoot contact that you'll continue on another channel, and then close the ticket — don't leave only an internal note with the ticket open and unanswered, even if you're continuing elsewhere.
